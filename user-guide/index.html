How to read NOMADTIPS3

This guide explains how to navigate the public site, read live football analysis, distinguish live indicators from validated signals, and understand the Statistics page without confusing pressure, probability and historical performance.

1. Start with the four public pages

The public navigation is deliberately simple. Each page has a different job and the information should not be treated as interchangeable.

Signal is for matches being monitored by the live Asian Handicap signal system.
Statistics reviews locked and settled signals after the event.
Live Score shows live match state, event flow and analytical indicators. Live analysis is not automatically a signal.
Picks presents curated pre-match selections and match analysis separately from the live engine.

2. How to read the Signal page

The Signal page summarizes the live scanner through four top-level states: live matches, watching, near signal and signal. Search and filtering can help narrow the monitored match list.

  • LIVE MATCHES — matches currently included in the live scan.
  • WATCHING — matches the selected-side detector is actively monitoring.
  • NEAR SIGNAL — a match is closer to the configured requirements but still waiting for a valid entry.
  • SIGNAL — a selected-side Asian Handicap signal has been locked after validation.
Fail-closed principle: when the required live Asian Handicap information is unavailable, the signal engine is designed not to substitute an unrelated 1X2, goals or corner market.

3. Live Score overview

The Live Score page is the analytical match-monitoring layer. It shows the health of the live feed and whether enough recent event history exists to calculate rolling analysis.

  • FRESH FEED means the incoming match information is not currently classified as stale.
  • ROLLING WINDOW means the system has enough recent snapshots to compare change over the configured period.
  • EVENT PASS means the configured event conditions passed at that moment. It is not automatically a betting signal.

4. NOMAD Live Prediction

The live outcome model displays a HOME WIN, DRAW and AWAY WIN estimate that totals 100%. It uses the current score and time together with available live evidence such as pressure, shots on target, dangerous attacks, attacks and corners.

A higher number means the model currently gives that outcome more weight than the others. It does not mean the result is guaranteed. The estimate can change as the score, time, pressure and event data change.

Do not confuse probability with pressure. HOME WIN 58% is an outcome-model estimate. HOME PRESSURE 58% is a rolling share of recent weighted attacking pressure. They measure different things.

5. Match Pulse, pressure and momentum

Match Pulse combines the current rolling pressure share with trend status, rolling-window information, Event Gate state and feed freshness.

HOME PRESSURE

This is the HOME share of weighted pressure in the current rolling window. It is designed to reflect recent attacking activity, not final-match probability.

TREND x/3

This shows how many configured trend conditions currently pass. It is not a confidence score.

Momentum Pulse and Pressure Shift

Momentum Pulse compares recent weighted attacking activity by interval. Pressure Shift summarizes the current rolling balance as HOME CONTROL, BALANCED or AWAY CONTROL. Taller or larger activity indicates stronger recent pressure, not a guaranteed goal.

6. Event Timeline, rolling battle bars and radar

The Event Timeline highlights recent stored changes such as shots on target, shots off target and corners. The rolling battle bars compare HOME and AWAY changes in attacks, dangerous attacks, shots, SOT and corners over the current window.

Remember that rolling values are changes during the selected window, not necessarily full-match totals. A display such as HOME ATTACKS +8 means the recorded attack total increased by eight during that window.

The Radar Comparison normalizes available rolling metrics so HOME and AWAY shapes can be compared. A larger radar area does not itself represent a probability of winning.

7. Reading the Statistics summary

The Statistics page reviews recorded live signals after they are locked and, where applicable, settled.

  • TOTAL SIGNALS — number of recorded signals in the applicable data set.
  • WIN RATE — calculated from settled signals, according to the system’s settlement logic.
  • AVG ODDS — average of the locked entry odds where available.
  • ROI — performance calculated using the standard one-unit reference method used by NOMADTIPS3.
Historical statistics are descriptive. A positive historical ROI or win rate does not guarantee the same result in future matches.

8. Cumulative Unit and Result Ledger

The Cumulative Unit chart uses settled results only. A green bar represents a positive unit result, a red bar represents a negative unit result, and each bar continues from the previous cumulative level. The dashed reference indicates the current cumulative unit after the latest settled result. One unit (1u) is a standard reference amount used to compare results consistently across different odds.

  • CONDITION identifies the configured condition or signal context where recorded.
  • PICK / AH identifies the selected side and Asian Handicap line.
  • ODDS / BOOKMAKER records the available market reference used by the system.
  • ENTRY is the score at or around the recorded signal entry.
  • FINAL is the final recorded score.
  • RESULT identifies settlement such as WIN, LOSS or PUSH.
  • P/L expresses the settled result using the unit reference.

9. A simple reading routine

Check the match time, score and feed freshness first.
Read recent pressure and actual event evidence together rather than relying on one number.
Keep HOME PRESSURE, LIVE PREDICTION and EVENT PASS separate in your interpretation.
If you are reviewing a Signal, check the selected side, Asian Handicap line, market reference and entry context.
Use Statistics to evaluate the historical record, not to assume the next match must repeat the past.
